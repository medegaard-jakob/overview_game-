How it works

Planes

A plane shows up at its scheduled arrival. If none is known, it comes 2½ h before departure, or earlier if its first ULD reaches the stand before that. It leaves at departure (the estimated time when there is one).

If the flight changed stand, the plane moves with it. Only one plane fits on a stand: the one that got there first stays until it leaves.

Planes use the runway the real wind decides and taxi on the official taxiways.

ULDs at stands

Delivered ULDs stay at the stand until their flight leaves (at most 3 h, or 45 min if the flight is unknown). Sometimes they wait without a plane, and a tag says why:

For UA26 aircraft on 246L dropped at the flight's old stand

For EI153 aircraft due 07:20 the plane has not arrived yet

A ULD counts as empty when it was picked up from an empty-ULD store; everything else is full. Full ULDs wait at their bank from when they are created until a tug collects them.

Tugs

The colour shows what the tug is doing:

Tugs follow the system log. Between two log entries they drive along the service roads, so a tug can be a minute or two behind the real moment.

Counters

ULDs delivered: dropped today up to now. Flights served: flights that got at least one ULD.

On-time delivery: share delivered before their latest finish time. Late: delivered after it. Urgent: urgent ULDs delivered. Escalated: ULDs made urgent after their order was created.

Map

Real: stand and taxiway positions (from the official Heathrow chart), the weather (Heathrow reports every 30 min) and day and night (the real sunrise and sunset).

Drawn for looks: the runway position, the buildings and the service roads.

Data

Everything comes from the GHS export: orders, ULDs, drivers, flights and their logs. Nothing is made up; where a time is missing, the rules above fill it in.

A checker compares the page with the raw export: counters, tug status, planes on stand and ULD piles.
